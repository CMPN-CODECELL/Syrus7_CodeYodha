# Limitations and trade-offs (honest list)

1. **One 021 account per installation, linked by the operator.** `gateway021` serves exactly the 021 account whose UCC and password are in `.env`. Which Countersign users may operate it is configured by email (`CS_021_OWNER_EMAILS`); there is no self-service linking of a trader's own 021 account (future scope). Everyone else trades on their own paper account.
2. **What the 021 sandbox does, as observed on 2026-10-09.** These are sandbox behaviours, not Countersign's, but they shape what a demo shows:
    - Limit orders were filled at their limit price even when it was far from the market (a buy limit about 10% below the last price filled within a minute), so a "resting" order cannot be relied on to stay open.
    - Orders that 021 confirmed as cancelled were later reported as executed. Countersign reports this as "Filled after its cancellation" and does not treat it as a violation, but a cancellation on 021 cannot be trusted to be final.
    - Some instruments carry two interleaved price series; the gateway publishes the median of the last 5 updates, which suppresses one-off spikes and lets a sustained move through after about 3 updates. IDEA moves by several percent between updates, so approvals on it often expire with `PRICE_DRIFT` (the 1% approval band working as designed).
    - Live prices are sometimes off the instrument's tick size; the verifier rounds protection prices to the tick and blocks any price the trader typed off the tick.
    - On Saturday 10 October NSE is closed and the sandbox may not move prices or fill orders. Standing-instruction demos can use paper accounts, whose prices the presenter feed scenarios can drive.
3. **Funds and margin on 021 are estimates.** The sandbox has no funds or margin endpoint. The estimate is the virtual Rs 10,00,000 daily funds minus open buy orders and today's net buys; 021's own risk checks decide, and a risk refusal is reported as a rejection.
4. **Instrument coverage.** The working set is the curated NSE stocks in the instrument master (AXISBANK, BHARTIARTL, HDFCBANK, ICICIBANK, IDEA, INFY, ITC, KOTAKBANK, LT, MARUTI, RELIANCE, SBIN, TATASTEEL, TCS, TMCV, TMPV, WIPRO, YESBANK), the NIFTY 50 and BANKNIFTY indices and NIFTY options for the nearest expiries. Orders were exercised live on NSE cash stocks only; BSE and F&O orders on 021 are not demonstrated. TATAMOTORS is not listed on 021 (it demerged into TMPV and TMCV), so the fictional TATAMOTORS, ABCCORP, XYZSUSP and the look-alike RELIANCE exist only in the replay feed for the Attack Lab and paper demos.
5. **Effectively once, not exactly once.** On the paper broker delivery is at-least-once with idempotent tags and reconcile-before-retry (at most one safe retry when the order is confirmed absent). On 021 nothing is retried, so a lost answer can end as "needs your review" (several matching orders, or an unreadable order book) instead of a definite answer; Countersign then never guesses and never resends.
6. **Plans.** A plan stops sending at the first step the broker refuses when it is sent (on 021 also a step that was not placed or needs review), and the remaining steps are skipped and reported; a rejection reported after a step was accepted does not stop independent steps. Rebalancing uses holdings only and only sells. Generated exit and rebalance plans are capped at 20 steps, dictated plans at 6.
7. **Standing instructions.** Standing orders watch one instrument; only alerts can watch every holding. The holdings alert measures the day's change from the previous close and sees a new holding within about 20 seconds. A condition that is already true when drafted is not armed; the copilot asks instead.
8. **Paper accounts and the replay feed.** With the gateway running, paper accounts trade on live 021 prices for real instruments; paper seed averages are rescaled to live closes when an account is created or reset, so an older paper account keeps its earlier averages until a reset. Presenter feed scenarios (spike, crash, dip) can drive a live instrument for up to two minutes for every paper account at once; they never affect the 021 account's orders or standing instructions. Without the gateway, every price comes from a deterministic replay with simple Black-Scholes option pricing. Charges and margin on paper are estimates from published schedules, labelled as estimates.
9. **Local accounts, no email.** Anyone who opens the app can sign up with a name, email and password, and gets their own paper trading account. Because everything runs locally with no mail server:
    - Emails are never verified; the email is only a unique sign-in name (and, for 021 linking, the key the operator lists).
    - There is no "forgot password" email. A forgotten PIN can be reset with the account password plus the authenticator code or passkey when the trader has one (Security Center), but a forgotten password means creating a new account. For a trader whose only approval method is the PIN, the password alone can reset it, which is why onboarding and the Security Center push for a passkey.
    - There is no KYC and no self-service account deletion. Test runs (`scripts/e2e_browser.py`) leave their throwaway `e2e.*@example.test` accounts in the local database.

    Sign-in (password or passkey) is never treated as an approval.
10. **Passkey prompts do not display the order.** WebAuthn authenticators cannot show transaction details (the transaction-display extensions were never shipped by browsers), so "what you see is what you sign" depends on the approval card, which is rendered only from the canonical object whose hash is signed.
11. **A passkey belongs to one host.** The WebAuthn RP ID is the hostname of the page (chosen per allowed origin), so passkeys work on `http://localhost:3000` and on an HTTPS origin listed in `CS_EXTRA_ORIGINS` (for example a Cloudflare tunnel), but a passkey registered on one host cannot be used on the other. IP addresses cannot have passkeys; on `127.0.0.1` the PIN and authenticator code still work.
12. **PIN and TOTP fallbacks are server-attested,** not device-signed, and are labelled with lower assurance.
13. **Small local model.** `qwen3:4b-instruct` runs on a 6 GB GPU and handles tool calls, but it can be slower or less fluent than hosted models. The deterministic engine guarantees every demo flow works without any model; safety never depends on the model.
14. **Regulatory scope.** Orders sent through broker APIs are treated as algo orders under SEBI's retail algo framework (in force since 1 April 2026); Countersign enforces the 10 orders/second threshold and gives information, not investment advice. Production use would need broker empanelment, static IP whitelisting and exchange algo tagging.
15. **Same Postgres role for all processes** in local development. Production would use separate database roles with least-privilege grants per process.
16. **Toxiproxy** is provided as an optional profile; the paper broker's fault injection and the gateway's two fault drills (`CS_021_TEST_FAULTS=true`) cover the demo scenarios. The sandbox's own injected faults (rate limits, 500/503, timeouts) cannot be switched on at will.
17. **Phones.** The UI is responsive and was checked at iPhone 15 and Pixel 7 sizes; the full browser journey, including passkey sign-in and countersign through Chrome's virtual authenticator, passes with `--device "Pixel 7"`. The web server, the API and the brokers listen on 127.0.0.1 only, so a phone reaches the app only through an HTTPS tunnel whose origin is in `CS_EXTRA_ORIGINS`. There is no native iOS or Android app.
18. **Some controls are deployment-wide on purpose.** The kill switch, the replay feed scenarios, broker fault injection and the Attack Lab sandboxes (LAB1, NAIVE1) are shared by every account on one installation. Any signed-in trader can engage the kill switch, and any trader can release it with their own PIN. A production deployment would restrict these to an operator role. The naive copilot counter on the Attack Lab scoreboard adds up across runs (5 per full run).
19. **Rate limits and the per-client login lockout live in the API process's memory.** They reset when the API restarts and are not shared between API replicas. Per-IP limits use the TCP peer and ignore `X-Forwarded-For` (the Next.js proxy passes a browser-supplied value through unchanged), so every browser behind the local proxy (and the tunnel) shares one bucket: a busy demo can briefly hit the sign-up (5/min) or sign-in (10/min) limits, and the per-client lockout cannot tell those browsers apart. A deployment behind a reverse proxy that sets a trusted client address header would key on that instead.
